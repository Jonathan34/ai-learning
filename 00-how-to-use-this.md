# How to Use This Material

## Format

Each chapter is written to be read in 8–12 minutes. Workshops are longer by design — they're meant for a weekend.

Every chapter follows roughly the same shape:

1. **The mental model** — what you need to actually hold in your head
2. **Why it matters** — what breaks in production if you skip this
3. **The details that experienced people know** — the things that don't show up in tutorials
4. **Gotchas** — common failure modes, misconceptions, bad advice you'll encounter
5. **What to read next** — if you want to go deeper

## Conventions

- **"This is the thing to remember"** — the key mental model, bolded
- **"Gotcha:"** — a specific trap with a specific cause
- **"Senior move:"** — something that separates someone with taste from someone with hype
- **"Honest status:"** — my read on how settled a topic is, because the field is uneven

## What "credible" means here

You'll see "credible" a lot. What I mean:

- You can design a system that works and explain *why* it will work, not just *that* it will work
- You can predict the failure modes before you ship
- You know what you don't know, and can say so without performing humility
- You don't get impressed or intimidated by buzzwords — you can translate "agentic" into concrete components
- You can work with ML engineers as peers, not as a client

The goal is not to become an ML researcher. The goal is to become the kind of engineer whose input ML engineers actively want in architecture discussions.

## Pacing

If you're doing the sprint path: two full days will cover Phase 1 + 2 reading and the three core workshops.

If you're doing the depth path: treat each chapter as a work session. Read, think, write down questions, come back tomorrow. You'll retain more.

## One piece of advice

**The hardest part of learning this is not the concepts. It's resisting the temptation to skim.**

A lot of AI content is fluff. Your filter from other domains is good — "is this person actually doing the work, or just talking about it?" Apply that filter here too. I've tried to keep fluff out. When a chapter seems obvious, trust that the 20% you're missing is the part that matters in production.

## On getting unstuck

If a concept lands wrong, you have three moves:

1. **Reread after sleeping.** Sometimes context clicks overnight.
2. **Build the concrete version.** Go to the relevant workshop.
3. **Look at a real production system.** Anthropic has published prompt engineering material; LangChain's docs have real patterns; the Claude Agent SDK docs are concrete. Reading other people's production prompts and eval harnesses is one of the fastest ways to build taste.

Don't get stuck perfecting one chapter. Keep moving — the chapters are designed to reinforce each other.
