# 19 — Staying Current

> Status: **outline**. Will expand in full depth.

## The mental model

The field moves fast. Most of what you read will be noise. The skill is not "read everything" but "filter effectively and go deep on what matters." This is a habit, not a one-time course.

## Planned contents

### What to follow
- Papers that matter (usually from a short list of labs: Anthropic, DeepMind, OpenAI, Meta AI, Google Research, some academic groups)
- Company blog posts from labs (often more actionable than papers)
- A small set of people on X / LinkedIn / Mastodon (not the hype accounts)
- A few newsletters (Import AI by Jack Clark, The Batch by Andrew Ng, Ahead of AI by Sebastian Raschka)
- Conference proceedings: NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ACL, occasional niche

### What to skip
- LinkedIn "AI agents will take your job" content
- Most Twitter threads with screenshots of prompts
- "Top 10 AI tools of 2026" listicles
- Vendor blog posts as primary sources
- AI-generated AI commentary

### Reading strategy
- Papers: abstract + introduction + conclusion first. Go deeper only if warranted.
- Blog posts from labs: often worth reading in full; they're doing the translation for you.
- Twitter threads: good signal when from researchers, poor signal otherwise.
- Follow implementations, not just papers. Code makes claims concrete.

### A self-sustaining loop
- Keep a small personal knowledge base of what you've learned
- Write (internal docs, blog posts, tweets) to force structure on what you think
- Teach one thing a month — the best test of whether you understand

### Tactical
- A recurring calendar slot for reading (2 hours a week minimum)
- Papers With Code, arxiv-sanity, HuggingFace Daily Papers as filters
- A curated list of 5-10 labs/researchers whose work you trust
- Skim a lot, read closely a little

## Key gotchas

- Firehose anxiety: you can't keep up with everything; don't try
- Benchmark drama: new SOTA on a benchmark is rarely actionable
- Hype amplification on social media
- Confusing novelty with importance
- Learning in breadth without depth — a little bit of everything, useful for nothing

## What a PE needs

- A sustainable reading and learning habit
- A small network of other AI practitioners to talk with
- Clear filter: "is this actually useful to my work, or just interesting?"
- The willingness to wait 3 months before adopting new techniques — most hype decays in that window
